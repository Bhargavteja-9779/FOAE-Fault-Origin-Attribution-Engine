\section{Datasets and cleaning}
All healthy telemetry is real and unmodified. Sessions are contiguous recordings after splitting at
backward time steps or gaps $>5$\,s and discarding sessions shorter than 120\,s. CANmodes
timestamps are repaired for an unpadded millisecond field, a seconds field that lags the
millisecond counter after each wrap, and locale thousands separators; rows with non-hexadecimal
identifiers are dropped. The four HCRL attack captures share one 482-s benign block, which is kept
once; attack segments are excluded. From can-train-and-test only the attack-free drives are used.
VED trips shorter than 150\,s or with fewer than 20 engine- and vehicle-speed samples are excluded,
and at most 40 trips per vehicle are used in the detection corpus.

\section{Cross-model generalization}
The test faults are generated by a structurally different process: a two-state (Gilbert--Elliott)
contact alternating between quiet periods and chatter episodes (mean 0.1\,s, 100 interruptions/s),
with exponentially distributed durations and a brown-out triggered when the \emph{cumulative} open
time of a supply-pin episode exceeds the hold-up time. Expected interruption rate and mean duration
equal those of the nominal Poisson model; temporal clustering, duration law and brown-out mechanism
differ. The detector is trained only on the nominal model.

\section{Reproducibility}
Software: Python 3.11, NumPy 2.4.6, pandas 3.0.6, SciPy 1.17.1, scikit-learn 1.9.1, LightGBM 4.7.0,
PyTorch 2.14.1 (CPU). Hardware: 4-core CPU, no GPU. Complete pipeline: \texttt{ocwd/get\_data.sh}
then \texttt{ocwd/run\_all.sh} (about 4--5\,h). Fourteen unit tests (\texttt{ocwd/tests}) cover the
timestamp repair, interruption statistics, loss mechanics, Poisson coherence test, Markov fault model
and baselines. Random seeds: three injection seeds (0, 1, 2) for all detection corpora; per-session
streams are derived from CRC32 of the session name, so results are independent of execution order.
