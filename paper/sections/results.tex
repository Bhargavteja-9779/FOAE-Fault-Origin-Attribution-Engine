Table~\ref{tab:detection} reports detection on all four corpora. Four
findings stand out.

\emph{(i) On full-bus captures ECTA generalizes to unseen vehicles.} Trained
on five vehicles and tested on the sixth, in turn, ECTA reaches an AUROC of
0.880 (95\% CI 0.871--0.906) for wear against \emph{all} other windows and a
true-positive rate of 55\% at a 1\% false-positive rate; no non-ECTA method
exceeds 0.653 AUROC or 29\% TPR@1\%. The end-to-end 1D-CNN and the LSTM
autoencoder, trained on the same windows, reach only 0.646 and 0.631: with
six vehicles whose buses differ in rate (1\,446--2\,570 frames/s), identifier
sets (27--98) and timing, we attribute this gap to raw sequence models
learning vehicle-specific patterns that the self-referenced ECTA features
normalize away.

\emph{(ii) ECTA is the only method that is strong on both questions.}
Single-statistic rules and novelty detectors are good at telling wear from
\emph{healthy} data---on full-bus captures the loss-rate threshold reaches
0.963 (moderate) and 0.996 (severe), above ECTA's 0.891 and 0.995---but they
collapse once confounders are present (0.56--0.67 overall for the two rules,
0.54--0.66 for the three novelty detectors, across corpora), because every
confounder also produces losses, timeouts or anomalies. ECTA trades a few
points of the easier question for a large gain on the realistic one; it has
the highest overall AUROC on the full-bus, polling (0.723) and fleet
(0.775, 95\% CI 0.772--0.778) corpora and ties the 1D-CNN on the sampling
corpus (0.675 vs.\ 0.677, overlapping CIs).

\emph{(iii) Detectability is governed by the wear stage and by the tool.}
Severe wear (6--30\,$\Omega$, where brown-outs begin) is detected against
healthy telemetry at every fidelity: 0.995 (full-bus), 0.957 (sampling),
0.891 (polling) and 0.851 (VED). Moderate wear (1.5--6\,$\Omega$) is
detected only by the full-bus logger (0.891, CI 0.878--0.964): its
interruptions last tens to hundreds of microseconds and destroy individual
frames, which only a tool that sees every frame can count. To a sampling
logger or a polling tester they are almost invisible (0.49--0.56), and
incipient wear is indistinguishable from healthy telemetry in any single
window for every method. Section~\ref{sec:obs} shows that this pattern is
predicted by first principles.

\emph{(iv) Excitation coherence is what makes polling-mode detection
work.} Removing the coherence features costs 0.037 AUROC on the polling
corpus and 0.071 on VED; on VED even ECTA's margin over its strongest
ablation (loss and coherence features only) has a 95\% CI of $+0.017$ to
$+0.019$. On full-bus captures it changes nothing (0.880 vs.\
0.878): when every frame is visible, the loss-structure features already
separate wear from its look-alikes. The training-free $z_\beta$ test alone is
not a detector: coherence is a \emph{discriminating} feature, informative in
combination with evidence that losses exist.

\subsection{An Observability Law}
\label{sec:obs}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig_observability.pdf}
\caption{Predicted observability SNR (Section~\ref{sec:method_obs}),
computed from the physics and each tool's measured healthy background
without any detector, against the best AUROC measured for that tool and
stage (I, M, S: incipient, moderate, severe wear).}
\label{fig:obs}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:obs} plots, for every tool and wear stage, the observability
SNR of Section~\ref{sec:method_obs}---the number of fault-induced events
the physics predicts per window, divided by the spread of the same event
count in the tool's \emph{real} healthy telemetry---against the best AUROC
any method achieved. The two are rank-correlated with Spearman
$\rho=0.93$ across the twelve tool--stage combinations. Every combination
with a predicted SNR above 0.8 is detected with AUROC $\ge 0.91$; every
combination below 0.4 stays at or below 0.59. The law explains the fidelity results
without reference to any classifier: a full-bus tool observes
$\sim$1\,600 frames/s and so turns moderate wear's sub-millisecond
interruptions into $\sim$45 lost frames per 30~s, whereas a polling tester
observing a few transactions per second expects about one fault-induced
event per two-minute window against a background spread of 34 such events
in healthy driving, until brown-outs begin. It also tells a tool designer what to change: the
observation rate, not the algorithm.

\subsection{Time to Detect With a Full-Bus Tool}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig_ttd.pdf}
\caption{Full-bus detection when one persistent fault is applied to an

accumulated (vehicle-disjoint models). Points are shown while at least 50
drive trajectories are long enough (399 at 0.5--2~min, 252 at 5~min, 105
at 10~min).}
\label{fig:ttd}
\end{figure}

A worn contact keeps its resistance for days, so a tool can accumulate
evidence. Fig.~\ref{fig:ttd} applies one fault per drive and stage to whole
real drives and averages the per-window log-odds. Severe wear is detected
against healthy drives with AUROC 0.98 after 30~s and 1.00 after one
minute; moderate wear reaches 0.94 after two minutes and 0.95 after five.
Even incipient wear begins to emerge (0.61 after five minutes, 0.76 after
ten on the 105 longest drives). In practice, a full-bus tool would flag a
moderately worn connector within the first few minutes of the first drive
after the wear develops.

Table~\ref{tab:conf} isolates the attribution question. Against bus-wide
EMI, coherence features raise the AUROC from 0.741 to 0.878 on the polling
corpus and from 0.737 to 0.947 on VED, even though half of the simulated
EMI was deliberately made ignition-coupled. The separation comes from the
joint footprint: DLC interruptions remove transactions and correlate with
\emph{both} engine and road excitation, whereas EMI delays transactions and
correlates, at most, with engine speed. Against vibration-independent loss,
coherence adds 0.031 on VED. Against an ECU-side intermittent no
telemetry feature helps in polling mode (0.73--0.76 for all methods): an
intermittent engine-ECU connector and an intermittent DLC both make Mode-01
responses disappear, both are vibration-driven, and a tester that talks
only to that ECU cannot tell them apart. On the full-bus corpus, where the
selectivity of losses across identifiers is observable, the same
distinction reaches 0.949 (and 0.979 against EMI, 0.977 against overflow,
versus 0.70--0.73 for the 1D-CNN). This is a structural limit worth stating
plainly: attributing a fault to the DLC rather than to an ECU connector
requires visibility of traffic from more than one ECU.

\subsection{Pooling Evidence Across Trips}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig_accumulation.pdf}
\caption{Vehicle-level detection on VED when the per-trip log-odds of $K$
trips of the same vehicle are averaged. Negatives include healthy and all
confounder trips.}
\label{fig:acc}
\end{figure}

A worn DLC stays worn, whereas the background irregularity of healthy
telemetry is trip-local. Fig.~\ref{fig:acc} exploits this. Averaging the
per-trip evidence of $K$ trips of the same vehicle raises severe-wear
detection on VED from 0.907 ($K{=}1$) to 0.987 ($K{=}3$) and 0.9998
($K{=}10$) against all negatives, and from 0.850 to 0.999 against healthy
vehicles alone. Pooling does not, however, create evidence that is not
there: moderate and incipient wear remain at chance against healthy
vehicles for every $K$ (0.46--0.50), confirming that for a
$\sim$1.3\,Hz polling logger their footprint lies below the logger's own
background irregularity (the healthy VED trips contain, on median, 31
gaps longer than 0.8\,s per minute).

The 166 VED vehicles with at least 60 trips over at least 200 days were
each given a connector that wears with that vehicle's own recorded usage
(21\,824 real trips). The learned health index tracks the true contact
resistance with $R^2=0.86$ (Spearman 0.77) on held-out vehicles
(Fig.~\ref{fig:prog}a), but its information is concentrated above
$\approx$2--4\,$\Omega$; below that it is left-censored, as the detection
results predict.

Table~\ref{tab:prog} reports the consequences honestly. With failure
pre-specified at the onset of the severe stage (6\,$\Omega$), the
detectability floor and the failure threshold nearly coincide, and
\emph{no} telemetry-based method beats the population reliability prior:
the RUL error is 64--71~days for all of them. We then evaluated the
threshold that matters operationally---functional failure of the tool link
at 20\,$\Omega$, where brown-outs dominate---noting that this second
threshold was chosen after seeing the first result. There, a simple alarm
on the trend of the learned health index warns 88\% of failing vehicles
in time (median lead 6.4 days) with a 1.5\% false-alarm rate on vehicles
that do not fail, whereas the fleet-reliability schedule, which uses
the same exposure information but no telemetry, is timely for only 36\%
and false-alarms on 61\%. Point estimates of RUL months ahead, by
contrast, are not improved by telemetry at this tool fidelity
(Fig.~\ref{fig:prog}b): the Bayesian exposure-domain estimator, which
correctly treats sub-floor readings as censored, matches the conditional
reliability model but does not beat it. Long-horizon RUL from telemetry
therefore requires a tool that sees moderate wear, i.e., full-bus capture.
Applying the detection thresholds established above to the same real
usage histories gives a projected warning window (from first reliable
detection to functional failure) of median 25.5 days (interquartile range
14.3--40.2) for a full-bus tool, which detects from about 1.5\,$\Omega$,
against 14.1 days (7.3--21.2) for a polling tool, which detects from about
6\,$\Omega$. This projection depends on the knee width of the wear law and
is therefore indicative, but the ordering does not.

\subsection{Additional Validation}
\label{sec:extra}
Four further checks, detailed in the Supplementary Material, address the
most likely objections to a physics-informed evaluation.

\emph{A structurally different fault model.} To test whether ECTA learns
the physics or merely the simulator, we generated test faults with a
different process: a two-state (Gilbert--Elliott) contact that alternates
between quiet periods and chatter episodes, with exponential rather than
log-normal interruption durations and a brown-out criterion based on the
\emph{cumulative} open time of an episode instead of a single long
interruption. Its mean interruption rate and duration equal those of the
nominal model; its temporal structure does not. Trained only on the nominal
model, ECTA reaches an AUROC of 0.856 on full-bus captures (moderate 0.848,
severe 0.999), against 0.880 on matched physics, and \emph{higher} values
than on matched physics for the polling corpus (0.779) and VED (0.813),
where the energy-based brown-out rule makes severe wear more visible.

\emph{Window length.} Full-bus detection rises monotonically with the
window: 0.824 (10~s), 0.879 (30~s) and 0.911 (60~s), with moderate wear at
0.843, 0.895 and 0.930. The 30-s window used throughout is therefore not
the most favourable choice; it trades a little accuracy for latency.

\emph{Hyperparameters.} Hyperparameters were fixed once and never tuned.
Across seven gradient-boosting configurations (200--1\,000 trees, 15--63
leaves, learning rates 0.02--0.1) the overall AUROC varies by at most
0.004 on full-bus captures, 0.007 on the polling corpus and 0.015 on VED.

\emph{Operating points.} With the threshold set to detect 90\% of severely
worn connectors, a full-bus tool raised \emph{no} false alarm on the real
healthy windows of held-out vehicles (0.39 per hour at 95\% detection).
For low-rate polling tools a single window cannot reach such a point, which
is why vehicle-level pooling (Fig.~\ref{fig:acc}) is required before acting.

\subsection{Feature Importance and Cost}
On VED the five most important features by split gain are the dispersion
of timeout-gap durations, the strong-event high/low excitation ratio, the
longest gap, long gaps per minute and the all-event excitation ratio
(Fig.~\ref{fig:imp}); coherence features account for
\input{tables/coh_share.tex}\unskip\% of the total gain. Feature extraction
takes 23~ms for a 30-s full-bus window containing 58\,583 frames and
4.8~ms for a 120-s polling window in unoptimized Python, and the trained
detector is a 1.3-MB tree ensemble; ECTA is therefore deployable on the
tool or at the fleet back end at negligible cost.
